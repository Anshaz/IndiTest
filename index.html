What does my score mean?

▼

Think of the score as a readiness meter. It does not tell you to buy blindly — it tells you how many layers of confirmation have aligned. The more layers, the higher your odds of a successful trade.

0–1
Wait
No setup. Momentum is weak or against you. Do not force a trade.
2
Building
Divergence or structure forming. Add to watchlist and stalk.
3
Plan entry
Momentum aligning. Prepare your stop-loss and position size.
4
Setup active
Strong signal. Enter only if ATR is compressed (room to run).
5
High conviction
Most layers aligned. Stop below higher low.
6
Max confluence
All 6 layers aligned. Stop below higher low.

Indicator guide for beginners

▼

1 VWAP — the fair-price anchor

What it measures: The average price that traders have actually paid, weighted by volume. Institutions use it as a "fair value" line.

Example: BBAI is at $2.84 and VWAP is at $2.85. Price is straddling the line — no clear bias yet. If BBAI gaps up to $3.10 and holds above VWAP at $2.90, that means the average buyer is now in profit. There is less overhead supply (people wanting to sell at break-even), so the path up is clearer.

What to look for: Price reclaiming VWAP from below on above-average volume = early long bias. Price rejecting VWAP from above = early short bias.

2 RSI Divergence — the early warning

What it measures: When price makes a lower low but RSI makes a higher low, it means selling pressure is weakening even though price is still dropping. This is exhaustion.

Example: BBAI drops from $3.00 to $2.50 to $2.40. But RSI reads 28 → 25 → 30. The second low in price ($2.40) is lower than the first ($2.50), yet RSI at 30 is higher than 25. Sellers are running out of ammunition. The next bounce is much more likely to hold.

What to look for: Price lower low + RSI higher low within the last 10 bars. This scores +1 in the app. It replaces the old noisy "RSI cross above SMA" rule.

3 Higher Low — market structure

What it measures: Actual price action. A higher low means buyers stepped in at a higher price than the previous dip. The downtrend is breaking structurally.

Example: BBAI makes a low at $2.20, bounces to $2.60, then pulls back to $2.35. The second low ($2.35) is higher than $2.20. Buyers are willing to buy sooner. That $2.35 level becomes your stop. If price breaks below it, the structure is broken and you are wrong.

What to look for: The most recent significant swing low is higher than the one before it. The app finds this automatically and suggests it as your stop level.

4 MACD — trend momentum

What it measures: The gap between a fast moving average (12 periods) and a slow one (26 periods). The histogram shows whether that gap is widening or shrinking.

Example: BBAI MACD histogram reads -0.39, then -0.28, then -0.15. The bars are getting less negative (shrinking red bars). Downward momentum is decelerating — like a ball rolling uphill and slowing down. When the histogram flips from red to green, that is the trend potentially reversing.

What to look for: Histogram negative and turning up for 2+ bars. This ensures you are buying a reversal, not chasing a move already in progress.

5 Volume — conviction check

What it measures: How many shares changed hands. Volume is the only indicator that cannot be faked — it shows real money moving.

Example: BBAI bounces 5% but volume is 12K vs its 9-day average of 45K. That bounce is a dead-cat bounce — no real buyers stepped in. A day later it falls again. Contrast with a 5% bounce on 80K volume: institutions are actually accumulating. That move has legs.

What to look for: Volume above its 9-day SMA on a green candle = conviction. Volume spike on a red candle = distribution (smart money selling). Volume below average on any move = low conviction, likely to fail.

6 ATR Compression — the coiled spring

What it measures: Average True Range tells you how much a stock typically moves per day. When ATR drops into the bottom 30% of its recent range, the stock is "coiled." Breakouts from compression have much higher follow-through.

Example: BBAI's daily range is usually $0.40 (ATR = 0.40). For the last 5 days, it has only moved $0.15 per day. ATR has compressed. When the next move comes — up or down — it is likely to be a big move because the energy has built up. Entering a long when ATR is compressed and the other 4 layers align means you catch the expansion, not the contraction.

What to look for: ATR(14) in the bottom 30% of its 50-day range. The app marks this as "compressed." Avoid entries when ATR is already expanded (top 30%) — that is when whipsaws happen.

Why two timeframes?

▼

Trading against the higher timeframe is like swimming against the current. The weekly chart tells you the trend. The daily chart tells you when to enter.

Weekly — the "wind direction"

We only check two things on the weekly chart:

  • Is price above weekly VWAP? → Trend is up
  • Is the weekly MACD histogram improving? → Momentum supporting the trend
Scenario: Weekly shows price above VWAP and MACD turning up. Daily score hits 4/5. This is a high-probability long because you are trading with the weekly trend.

Counter-scenario: Weekly shows price below VWAP. Daily score hits 4/5. This is a counter-trend bounce — it might work, but your stop must be tighter and your position smaller because the bigger trend is against you.

Daily — the "entry door"

This is where the 6-layer score lives. It tells you if the short-term setup is ready.

  • Score 0–1: No trade. Go outside.
  • Score 2–3: Watchlist. Divergence or structure forming. Set alerts.
  • Score 4: Setup active. ATR compressed? Define stop + target.
  • Score 5: Execute. All layers aligned. Stop below higher low.
Rule of thumb: Only take 5/6 or 6/6 setups when weekly trend is also bullish. If weekly is bearish, only take 6/6 with half position size and a very tight stop.

Speculative vs. Stable stocks

▼

A low-float AI stock like BBAI moves differently than a mega-cap like Apple. The same indicator thresholds would be too strict for one and too loose for the other. The app adjusts automatically.

Speculative Low-float, volatile

Examples: BBAI, MARA, RIVN, small-cap biotech

  • RSI overbought threshold: 75 (these stocks can run to 80+)
  • Volume needs: just above average (1.0x SMA)
  • HVN proximity: ±10% of range (price whipsaws more)
  • MACD: histogram just needs to stop falling
  • Weekly trend: optional — these can move against the trend
  • ATR compression: bottom 40% (they compress less often)
Why: Speculative stocks gap 10–20% overnight. Waiting for perfect confluence means missing the move. The app is more lenient so you catch the early momentum, but you must use tighter stops because reversals are brutal.

Stable High-float, less volatile

Examples: AAPL, MSFT, JNJ, SPY

  • RSI overbought threshold: 60 (moves are slower, exhaustion comes earlier)
  • Volume needs: 1.2x SMA (needs stronger conviction to break out)
  • HVN proximity: ±5% of range (price respects levels more)
  • MACD: histogram must show clear upward turn
  • Weekly trend: required — counter-trend moves rarely pay
  • ATR compression: bottom 25% (they compress more reliably)
Why: Mega-caps grind. A 5% move takes weeks. False breakouts are common, so the app demands stronger confirmation before alerting. But when they do break, they tend to follow through — wider stops work better here.

Volume 55K vs 35K SMA (✓). Higher low structure at $2.40 vs prior $2.20 (✓). ATR compressed to bottom 35% of range (✓).

Score: 6/6 on daily, weekly bullish, ATR compressed.
Action: Enter at $2.50. Stop at $2.35 (below higher low). Target $3.20 (next LVN). Risk $0.15, reward $0.70 = 1:4.7 R:R.

✗ Trap — no divergence (1/5)

BBAI at $3.10 after a sharp green candle.

Weekly: Price below weekly VWAP. Bearish trend.
Daily: Price $3.10, VWAP $2.95 (above ✓). RSI 72, no divergence — price and RSI both made lower highs (✗). MACD hist +0.05 but falling (✗). Volume 22K vs 40K SMA (✗). No higher low structure (✗). ATR expanded (✗).

Score: 1/6 on daily, weekly bearish.
Action: This is a bear-market rally with no exhaustion signal. The one green candle looks tempting but there is no divergence, no volume, and no structure. Next day it gaps down to $2.70. Waiting saved you $0.40/share.

⚠ Partial setup — stalk (3/5)

AAPL at $185 after earnings.

Weekly: Price above VWAP. Bullish.
Daily: Price $185, VWAP $183 (above ✓). RSI 58, divergence forming but not confirmed yet (✗). MACD hist -0.3 → -0.2 (negative & turning up ✓). Volume 85M vs 80M SMA (barely above, marginal ✓). Higher low at $182 vs $178 (✓). ATR expanded (top 25% of range) (✗).

Score: 3/6 on daily, weekly bullish.
Action: Structure and momentum are aligning, but ATR is already expanded. The stock has already moved. Set an alert for ATR to compress (usually 3–5 days of tight range) and re-check. Entering now risks buying the top of the move.

Quick reference

Before every trade, answer these 4 questions:
1. What is the weekly trend telling me? (Trade with it, not against it)
2. What is my daily score? (5+ for entry, 2 or less for exit)
3. Is ATR compressed? (Bottom 30% = room to run. Top 30% = avoid)
4. Where is my stop? (Below the higher low — the app suggests one)
