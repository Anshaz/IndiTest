What does my score mean?

▼

Think of the score as a readiness meter. It does not tell you to buy blindly — it tells you how many layers of confirmation have aligned. The more layers, the higher your odds of a successful trade.

0–1
Wait
No setup. Momentum is weak or against you. Do not force a trade.
2
Building
Something is shifting. Add to watchlist and set price alerts.
3
Plan entry
Momentum aligning. Prepare your stop-loss and position size.
4
Setup active
Strong signal. Enter only if price is near a support zone (HVN).
5
Execute
All layers aligned. Enter with stop below last higher low or HVN.

Indicator guide for beginners

▼

1 VWAP — the fair-price anchor

What it measures: The average price that traders have actually paid, weighted by volume. Institutions use it as a "fair value" line.

Example: BBAI is at $2.84 and VWAP is at $2.85. Price is straddling the line — no clear bias yet. If BBAI gaps up to $3.10 and holds above VWAP at $2.90, that means the average buyer is now in profit. There is less overhead supply (people wanting to sell at break-even), so the path up is clearer.

What to look for: Price reclaiming VWAP from below on above-average volume = early long bias. Price rejecting VWAP from above = early short bias.

2 RSI — momentum speedometer

What it measures: How fast and how far price has moved recently. Scaled 0–100. Below 30 = oversold (potential bounce). Above 70 = overbought (potential pullback).

Example: BBAI RSI drops to 28 (oversold) then curls back up to 35 and crosses above its own moving average (the purple line). That is a bullish divergence — momentum is turning before price has fully recovered. It is like a car that stopped skidding and is now gripping the road again.

What to look for: RSI crossing above its SMA while still under 65 = momentum turning up, but not yet overbought. If RSI is already 78, even a cross is suspect — the move may be exhausted.

3 MACD — trend momentum

What it measures: The gap between a fast moving average (12 periods) and a slow one (26 periods). The histogram shows whether that gap is widening or shrinking.

Example: BBAI MACD histogram reads -0.39, then -0.28, then -0.15. The bars are getting less negative (shrinking red bars). Downward momentum is decelerating — like a ball rolling uphill and slowing down. When the histogram flips from red to green, that is the trend potentially reversing.

What to look for: Histogram turning up from negative = sellers are losing control. Do not buy just because histogram is green — it can stay green while price drifts sideways.

4 Volume — conviction check

What it measures: How many shares changed hands. Volume is the only indicator that cannot be faked — it shows real money moving.

Example: BBAI bounces 5% but volume is 12K vs its 9-day average of 45K. That bounce is a dead-cat bounce — no real buyers stepped in. A day later it falls again. Contrast with a 5% bounce on 80K volume: institutions are actually accumulating. That move has legs.

What to look for: Volume above its 9-day SMA on a green candle = conviction. Volume spike on a red candle = distribution (smart money selling). Volume below average on any move = low conviction, likely to fail.

5 Volume Profile (HVN) — where liquidity lives

What it measures: Which price levels have traded the most volume over the lookback period. Thick bars = High-Volume Nodes (HVN) where price tends to "stick." Thin gaps = Low-Volume Nodes (LVN) where price moves fast.

Example: BBAI's volume profile shows the heaviest trading happened between $3.74–$4.77. That is an overhead supply shelf — lots of people bought there and are waiting to break even. If you buy at $2.84 (thin zone below), your target on the way up is that $3.74+ shelf. Your stop should be below the nearest HVN support, say $2.60.

What to look for: Entering long near an HVN below current price = buying at a support zone with real liquidity. Entering in a thin zone = no safety net if price drops.

Why two timeframes?

▼

Trading against the higher timeframe is like swimming against the current. The weekly chart tells you the trend. The daily chart tells you when to enter.

Weekly — the "wind direction"

We only check two things on the weekly chart:

  • Is price above weekly VWAP? → Trend is up
  • Is the weekly MACD histogram improving? → Momentum supporting the trend
Scenario: Weekly shows price above VWAP and MACD turning up. Daily score hits 4/5. This is a high-probability long because you are trading with the weekly trend.

Counter-scenario: Weekly shows price below VWAP. Daily score hits 4/5. This is a counter-trend bounce — it might work, but your stop must be tighter and your position smaller because the bigger trend is against you.

Daily — the "entry door"

This is where the 5-layer score lives. It tells you if the short-term setup is ready.

  • Score 0–1: No trade. Go outside.
  • Score 2–3: Watchlist. Set alerts.
  • Score 4: Setup active. Define stop + target.
  • Score 5: Execute. Risk/reward should be 1:2 or better.
Rule of thumb: Only take 4/5 or 5/5 setups when weekly trend is also bullish. If weekly is bearish, only take 5/5 with half position size and a very tight stop.

Speculative vs. Stable stocks

▼

A low-float AI stock like BBAI moves differently than a mega-cap like Apple. The same indicator thresholds would be too strict for one and too loose for the other. The app adjusts automatically.

Speculative Low-float, volatile

Examples: BBAI, MARA, RIVN, small-cap biotech

  • RSI overbought threshold: 75 (these stocks can run to 80+)
  • Volume needs: just above average (1.0x SMA)
  • HVN proximity: ±10% of range (price whipsaws more)
  • MACD: histogram just needs to stop falling
  • Weekly trend: optional — these can move against the trend
Why: Speculative stocks gap 10–20% overnight. Waiting for perfect confluence means missing the move. The app is more lenient so you catch the early momentum, but you must use tighter stops because reversals are brutal.

Stable High-float, less volatile

Examples: AAPL, MSFT, JNJ, SPY

  • RSI overbought threshold: 60 (moves are slower, exhaustion comes earlier)
  • Volume needs: 1.2x SMA (needs stronger conviction to break out)
  • HVN proximity: ±5% of range (price respects levels more)
  • MACD: histogram must show clear upward turn
  • Weekly trend: required — counter-trend moves rarely pay
Why: Mega-caps grind. A 5% move takes weeks. False breakouts are common, so the app demands stronger confirmation before alerting. But when they do break, they tend to follow through — wider stops work better here.

Real trade scenarios

▼

✓ Good long setup

BBAI at $2.50 after a 3-week downtrend.

Weekly: Price just reclaimed weekly VWAP. MACD histogram flattened.
Daily: Price $2.50, VWAP $2.42 (above ✓). RSI 32 → crossed above SMA at 36 (✓). MACD hist -0.15 → -0.08 → -0.02 (turning up ✓). Volume 55K vs 35K SMA (✓). Price sits on HVN at $2.48 (✓).

Score: 5/5 on daily, weekly trend bullish.
Action: Enter at $2.50. Stop at $2.35 (below HVN). Target $3.20 (next LVN). Risk $0.15, reward $0.70 = 1:4.7 R:R.

✗ Trap — why you wait

BBAI at $3.10 after a sharp green candle.

Weekly: Price below weekly VWAP. Bearish trend.
Daily: Price $3.10, VWAP $2.95 (above ✓). RSI 72, no cross (✗). MACD hist +0.05 but falling (✗). Volume 22K vs 40K SMA (✗). Price far from any HVN (✗).

Score: 1/5 on daily, weekly trend bearish.
Action: This is a bear-market rally. The one green candle looks tempting but there is no volume, no momentum, and no liquidity support. Next day it gaps down to $2.70. Waiting saved you $0.40/share.

⚠ Partial setup — stalk, don't enter

AAPL at $185 after earnings.

Weekly: Price above VWAP. Bullish.
Daily: Price $185, VWAP $183 (above ✓). RSI 58, crossed above SMA (✓). MACD hist -0.3 → -0.2 (turning up ✓). Volume 85M vs 80M SMA (barely above, marginal ✓). Price is at $185 but HVN is at $182 — 3% away (✗ for stable profile).

Score: 3/5 on daily, weekly bullish.
Action: Not a full setup. Price is not at a liquidity node. Set an alert for $182 (the HVN) and wait. If it pulls back there with score still 3+, that becomes a 4/5 or 5/5 entry. Patience turns a 3 into a 5.

Quick reference

Before every trade, answer these 4 questions:
1. What is the weekly trend telling me? (Trade with it, not against it)
2. What is my daily score? (4+ for entry, 2 or less for exit)
3. Where is my stop? (Below the last higher low or HVN — never a fixed %)
4. What is my target? (Next LVN, overhead HVN, or fixed 1:2 R:R minimum)
